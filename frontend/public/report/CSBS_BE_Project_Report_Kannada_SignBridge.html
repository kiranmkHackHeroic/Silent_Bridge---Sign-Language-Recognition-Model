ABSTRACT (SYNOPSIS)
Mandatory Concise Synopsis (≤ 100 Words as per CSBS Guideline 5.d)

Kannada SignBridge is a real-time, privacy-preserving bidirectional assistive platform connecting Indian Sign Language (ISL) signers and Kannada speakers on commodity hardware. Using in-browser MediaPipe hand/face landmarks (147-D hands-face-v2 schema), motion segmentation, and a Bidirectional GRU with 3-view Test-Time Augmentation (TTA), isolated-sign accuracy on unseen signers improved from 54.5% to 76.5% (p=0.003, overall 81.5%, macro-F1 0.800). A fused BiGRU-CTC and sentence classifier decodes continuous ISL sentences, while a multi-tier SOV grammar engine and a procedural 3D Pixar-style avatar with analytic inverse kinematics translate Kannada text back into continuous ISL signing with zero mid-sign arm drops.

Extended Technical Overview

Deaf and hard-of-hearing citizens in Karnataka predominantly communicate in Indian Sign Language (ISL), whereas public institutions, hospitals, banks, and schools operate in Kannada. Existing sign recognition prototypes often evaluate on signer-dependent random splits, require GPU servers that expose private camera video, ignore regional Dravidian languages, and lack reverse text-to-sign synthesis. Kannada SignBridge addresses these gaps through a dual-engine architecture (FastAPI/Express WebSocket backend plus a 100% in-browser WebAssembly ONNX Runtime engine), few-shot custom sign personalization via block-balanced cosine similarity, a 187-phrase SOV meaning engine, and a pre-registered promotion gate that prevents model regressions.

CHAPTER 1
INTRODUCTION
1.1 Background and Context of the Project

Sign languages are complete, self-contained natural languages possessing their own phonological, morphological, syntactic, and discourse structures; they are not manual encodings or word-for-word gestural representations of spoken languages [1], [5]. Across India, Indian Sign Language (ISL) serves as the primary visual-spatial vernacular for millions of deaf and hard-of-hearing citizens. According to the Census of India and estimates from the National Association of the Deaf (NAD), India is home to over 5 million deaf and hard-of-hearing individuals, with more than 300,000 residing in the state of Karnataka alone. Despite the linguistic richness of ISL, fewer than 350 certified ISL interpreters are registered nationwide by the Indian Sign Language Research and Training Centre (ISLRTC), creating an acute communication barrier whenever a deaf citizen interacts with public infrastructure.

In Karnataka, this communication divide is compounded by a regional linguistic mismatch. While the deaf community communicates in ISL, the surrounding hearing population—including hospital receptionists, primary care physicians, bank officers, municipal clerks, transport staff, and school teachers—reads and speaks Kannada, a classical Dravidian language written in the Kannada abugida script (Unicode block U+0C80–U+0CFF). Virtually all mainstream sign language recognition research has concentrated on American Sign Language (WLASL [1], MS-ASL [2]), German Sign Language (RWTH-PHOENIX-Weather [3]), or Chinese Sign Language, outputting English or German text. Even the limited Indian Sign Language prototypes developed in academic literature predominantly output English glosses rather than grammatically well-formed regional Indian languages.

1.2 Project Domain Overview

The engineering domain of Kannada SignBridge sits at the intersection of five technical disciplines: (i) Visual-Spatial Sign Linguistics, (ii) Real-Time Pose and Landmark Computer Vision, (iii) Spatio-Temporal Deep Sequence Modeling, (iv) Dravidian Computational Linguistics and SOV Machine Translation, and (v) Procedural 3D Character Rigging and Analytic Inverse Kinematics.

From a linguistic perspective, an ISL sign is defined simultaneously along five phonological parameters originally formalized by Stokoe and extended to ISL [5], [19]: (1) Handshape (the configuration and flexion of the 20 finger joints relative to the wrist), (2) Location (the spatial placement of the hands relative to the signer’s face, chin, forehead, or chest), (3) Palm Orientation (the 3D unit normal of the palmar surface and the longitudinal axis of the middle finger), (4) Movement (the trajectory, velocity, and path length of the wrists over time), and (5) Non-Manual Markers (NMMs) (eyebrow elevation, brow furrow, head tilt, head shake, and lip mouthing that encode interrogative, negative, or declarative sentence mood). Many distinct ISL signs share identical handshapes and differ solely in their location relative to the face—for example, EAT, DRINK, WATER, SPEAK, and NOSE all occur within centimeters of the mouth and nose. Consequently, a recognition architecture that strips face-relative coordinates or fails when a hand momentarily occludes the face cannot reliably separate these high-frequency everyday signs.

Furthermore, translating between ISL and Kannada requires bridging two structurally distinct grammars. While both ISL and Kannada exhibit Subject–Object–Verb (SOV) tendencies, ISL is topic-prominent, drops copulas and case inflections, places wh-interrogatives and negation markers at the clause periphery accompanied by non-manual facial grammar, and orders temporal expressions before locatives. Kannada, by contrast, is a highly agglutinative Dravidian language where grammatical relations are expressed by suffixing locative (-ಅಲ್ಲಿ / -alli), dative (-ಗೆ / -ge), and accusative postpositions directly onto nominal stems and inflecting verbs for tense, aspect, and person-number-gender agreement.

1.3 Motivation

Four concrete real-world observations motivated the creation of Kannada SignBridge:

  1. Severe Interpreter Scarcity in Regional Languages: While urban English-speaking deaf individuals can occasionally access remote interpretation, a deaf patient visiting a taluk hospital or a student attending a Kannada-medium school in Karnataka has no practical access to real-time ISL–Kannada interpretation.
  2. Illusion of Accuracy from Signer-Dependent Splits: Many published ISL recognition studies evaluate models by randomly splitting recordings of the same signer across training and test sets, reporting >95% accuracy that collapses below 50% when tested on a new person. A deployable assistive tool must be evaluated strictly on unseen signers.
  3. Privacy and Bandwidth Constraints of Video Cloud Uploads: Streaming raw webcam video to remote GPU servers violates user privacy in medical or financial consultations and fails on low-bandwidth rural connections. Extracting compact 3D landmarks in the browser and running ONNX inference locally eliminates video transmission entirely.
  4. One-Way Communication is Half a Conversation: Recognising a deaf person’s signs into Kannada only solves one direction; the hearing person’s Kannada reply must also be translated back into continuous, natural-looking 3D ISL signing.

1.4 Problem Statement

“Given a standard commodity laptop equipped only with an integrated webcam and a CPU, design and implement a privacy-preserving, bidirectional assistive communication system that (i) accurately recognises isolated Indian Sign Language (ISL) signs and closed-set continuous ISL sentences performed by previously unseen signers and translates them into grammatically coherent Kannada text and speech with calibrated uncertainty rejection, (ii) supports instant few-shot personalization for user-specific custom signs, and (iii) converts Kannada text into continuous, co-articulated ISL signing rendered on an expressive 3D avatar retargeted from authentic ISL dataset recordings, while enforcing a statistical model-governance gate that prevents accuracy regressions.”

1.5 Aim and Objectives

The overarching aim of this project is to build, benchmark, and validate a production-grade, signer-independent ISL–Kannada bidirectional communication platform running entirely on commodity hardware. To achieve this aim, eight specific, measurable technical objectives were established:

  1. Objective 1 — Privacy-First Landmark Feature Engineering: Design a 147-dimensional per-frame feature representation (hands-face-v2) extracted locally in the browser via MediaPipe Hand and Face Landmarkers, capturing wrist-centered palm-normalized hand shape, image position, face-relative location, orthonormal palm orientation, and visibility flags without ever transmitting camera video frames.
  2. Objective 2 — Signer-Independent Isolated Sign Recognition: Train and evaluate a Bidirectional GRU (BiGRU) sequence classifier augmented with 3-view Test-Time Augmentation (TTA), face-occlusion landmark imputation, and selective HTTP-range expansion from the INCLUDE corpus to exceed 75% top-1 accuracy on completely unseen signers.
  3. Objective 3 — Calibrated Uncertainty & Non-Sign Rejection: Implement out-of-fold validation threshold calibration (τ*) so low-confidence or out-of-distribution movements are flagged as uncertain with top-k alternatives rather than silently corrupting the Kannada transcript.
  4. Objective 4 — Continuous ISL Sentence Decoding: Develop a fused sequence decoder combining a two-layer BiGRU trained with Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) and a sentence-level classifier over the 101-sentence ISL-CSLTR corpus, evaluated on unseen recording rounds.
  5. Objective 5 — Multi-Tier Kannada SOV Meaning & Speech Synthesis: Build a linguistic translation layer combining a 187-entry phrase dictionary, deterministic SOV constituent reordering with Kannada case postpositions, facial non-manual grammar modulation, and native Kannada (kn-IN) speech synthesis.
  6. Objective 6 — Expressive 3D Pixar-Style Avatar with Continuous IK Signing: Construct a procedural Three.js 3D character rig with analytic two-bone inverse kinematics, Gaussian-smoothed dataset-retargeted clips, ballistic co-articulation transitions, and Kannada phonetic viseme lip-mouthing that eliminates mid-sign arm drops.
  7. Objective 7 — Zero-Backend In-Browser ONNX & Few-Shot Custom Signs: Export the trained BiGRU ensemble to ONNX for 100% client-side WebAssembly inference and enable instant few-shot custom sign registration using block-balanced cosine similarity.
  8. Objective 8 — Statistical Model Governance & Promotion Gating: Enforce a pre-registered promotion gate using exact paired McNemar tests on identical held-out videos with one-click version rollback.

1.6 Scope of the Project

The functional and technical scope of Kannada SignBridge encompasses the complete lifecycle of bidirectional ISL–Kannada communication, dataset curation, model training, evaluation governance, and edge deployment:

  • Authentic ISL Vocabulary & Corpora: The system operates exclusively on authentic Indian Sign Language recordings sourced from three peer-reviewed CC BY 4.0 datasets: Amrita SLR (933 validated videos across 49 signs and 7 signer groups), INCLUDE (816 validated videos across 42 core classes, plus 1,430 selectively downloaded videos covering 144 total vocabulary classes), and ISL-CSLTR (492 validated sentence videos across 101 sentences and 168 glosses).
  • Bidirectional Translation & Multimodal Output: Forward translation supports isolated sign streams (with motion-based segmentation, still-tail trimming, and motion-minimum dip splitting) and closed-set continuous sentences, producing Unicode NFC Kannada text, English glosses, and spoken Kannada audio. Reverse translation converts arbitrary Kannada text into a planned sequence of ISL glosses animated continuously on a 3D avatar across 161 supported vocabulary clips.
  • Dual Runtime Execution Modes: Users can run inference either via the full-featured WebSocket backend (supporting live diagnostics, dataset recording, and model training) or via the 100% In-Browser ONNX WebAssembly Engine, which executes the 3,668-dimensional BiGRU model directly in the browser with zero server dependency.
  • Few-Shot Personalization Studio: A dedicated Custom Signs studio allows users to record 3 to 5 webcam samples of any new regional or personal sign, compute a 3,668-D outlier-resistant prototype centroid in browser storage, and immediately recognise the custom sign alongside the base vocabulary.
1.7 Significance and Expected Contribution

This project makes seven concrete engineering and scientific contributions to assistive computing for Indian languages:

  1. End-to-End Bidirectional ISL–Kannada Pipeline: Bridges visual-spatial ISL and Dravidian Kannada across word, phrase, and sentence levels in both directions within a single offline-capable web application.
  2. Selective HTTP-Range Dataset Expansion Algorithm: Introduces a CRC-32-verified HTTP range downloader that extracts only vocabulary-matching videos from 56.8 GB of remote INCLUDE ZIP archives (downloading 5.96 GB instead of 44.7 GB), boosting unseen-signer accuracy by +9.4 percentage points (p = 0.003).
  3. 3-View Test-Time Augmentation & Face-Occlusion Resilience: Combines full-segment, 3-tap zero-phase denoised, and 84% core-stroke views with EMA face-anchor tracking and median face-relative coordinate imputation to maintain high accuracy during hand-over-face signs.
  4. Fused BiGRU-CTC and Sentence Classifier Decoder: Achieves 29.2% exact-sentence accuracy on unseen recording rounds of the 101-sentence ISL-CSLTR benchmark (≈ 29× random chance), outperforming standalone CTC (22.1%) and standalone classification (20.1%).
  5. Artifact-Free 3D Avatar Retargeting & Procedural Character Rig: Cleans raw dataset landmarks via gap interpolation and σ=1.5 Gaussian smoothing—eliminating all 367 mid-sign arm drops across 144 clips and reducing wrist acceleration jitter by 36%—and renders them on a Pixar-inspired 3D character with analytic 2-bone IK and Kannada phonetic viseme mouthing.
  6. Pre-Registered Promotion Gate & Negative Result Transparency: Demonstrates rigorous ML governance by automatically rejecting two candidate models (including an uncurated 144-class expansion that diluted unseen-signer accuracy from 76.5% to 70.9%).
  7. Six-Stage Live Pipeline Diagnostics & Error Attribution: Provides real-time introspection into camera FPS, landmark presence, feature parity, segmentation boundaries, confidence calibration, and translation rules.

1.10 Constraints and Limitations

In accordance with rigorous engineering practice, the design and evaluation of Kannada SignBridge explicitly account for six practical constraints and operational boundaries:

  1. Authentic Public Dataset Scarcity and Signer Diversity: Unlike American Sign Language benchmarks that contain tens of thousands of clips across hundreds of signers, publicly licensed ISL datasets with verified class labels contain roughly 17 to 45 valid recordings per sign across 7 to 12 signers. Furthermore, Amrita SLR was recorded in Kerala and INCLUDE in Chennai; regional lexical variations signed in parts of Karnataka may differ from the training corpus unless adapted via the Few-Shot Custom Signs module or the Sign Reference guide.
  2. Commodity CPU & Browser Memory Envelope: Both training and live inference were constrained to run on a standard CPU-only laptop without discrete NVIDIA CUDA GPUs. Consequently, massive vision backbones (such as I3D, VideoMAE, or SlowFast) were ruled out in favor of compact, mathematically structured 147-D landmark representations and Bidirectional GRUs.
  3. Monocular 2D-to-3D Depth Estimation in Avatar Retargeting: Dataset videos are recorded with single RGB cameras without depth sensors or motion-capture suits. While MediaPipe provides relative hand depth and our retargeting pipeline estimates distance from apparent palm scale (spalm) and solves elbow posture via analytic two-bone inverse kinematics, the resulting 3D trajectories remain approximations of full upper-body motion capture and are transparently badged as dataset-derived unverified clips until reviewed by native ISL signers.
  4. Closed-Set Continuous Sentence Decoding: The continuous sentence recognition pipeline (fusing BiGRU-CTC with a sentence classifier) is trained on the 101 sentences of the ISL-CSLTR corpus. While highly effective for those 101 conversational sentences (29.2% exact match on unseen rounds), open-vocabulary continuous sign translation requires orders-of-magnitude larger gloss-annotated parallel corpora.
  5. Continuous Signing Without Hand Lowering: In isolated-sign stream mode, boundaries are detected via motion stillness (280 ms), hand drop, or motion-minimum dips. When a signer performs rapid back-to-back signs without pausing or lowering the hands, consecutive signs can merge into a single segment (increasing Sign Error Rate).
  6. Camera Framing and Face Visibility Requirement: Because face-relative hand coordinates (FACEREL_OFFSET) carry critical discriminative power for signs performed near the head, hiding the signer’s face degrades accuracy from 66.8% to 47.8% in perturbation tests. Although our FaceTracker EMA and median imputation recover brief 1–45 frame hand-over-face occlusions, the user must initially frame both their upper torso and face within the webcam viewport.

2.1 Introduction

Automatic Sign Language Recognition (SLR), Sign Language Translation (SLT), and 3D Signing Avatar synthesis have evolved rapidly over the past decade, transitioning from sensor-instrumented data gloves and hidden Markov models to deep spatio-temporal networks operating on monocular video and skeletal pose landmarks. However, a critical examination of the literature reveals a sharp disparity between resource-rich Western sign languages (such as American Sign Language and German Sign Language) and Indian Sign Language (ISL)—and an even wider chasm when regional Indian languages such as Kannada are required as the target communication medium.

This chapter presents a systematic review of twenty-two foundational and state-of-the-art research studies organized across four thematic pillars: (i) Benchmark Sign Language Datasets and Split Protocols, (ii) Appearance-Based versus Pose-Landmark Feature Extraction, (iii) Isolated and Continuous Sequence Modeling with Neural Translation, and (iv) 3D Signing Avatars, Linguistic Notation, and Statistical Evaluation Governance. Section 2.3 synthesizes these studies into comparative matrices, and Section 2.4 formalizes the seven research and application gaps addressed by Kannada SignBridge.

2.2 Review of Existing Systems / Research Studies
2.2.1 Global and Indian Sign Language Datasets

1. Word-Level American Sign Language Benchmarks — Li et al. (WLASL, WACV 2020) [1] and Vaezi Joze & Koller (MS-ASL, BMVC 2019) [2]: Li et al. [1] introduced WLASL, a large-scale isolated ASL dataset comprising 21,083 videos across 2,000 glosses performed by 119 signers, comparing 2D/3D convolutional networks (I3D) and pose-based recurrent networks. Similarly, Vaezi Joze and Koller [2] curated MS-ASL with over 25,000 annotated videos across 1,000 signs from 222 signers, emphasizing signer-independent generalization under uncontrolled lighting and background variation. Both works demonstrated that pose-based and 3D-CNN models suffer substantial accuracy drops when evaluated on unseen signers compared to signer-overlapping splits, establishing signer-independent evaluation as a mandatory standard.

2. Continuous German Sign Language — Koller, Forster & Ney (RWTH-PHOENIX-Weather, CVIU 2015) [3]: Koller et al. [3] established the benchmark for continuous sign language recognition (CSLR) using weather forecast broadcasts in German Sign Language (DGS) with frame-level and gloss-level annotations across multiple signers. While RWTH-PHOENIX catalyzed deep CTC research, its domain is restricted to weather broadcasts in DGS and relies on studio-grade broadcast video.

3. Indian Sign Language Isolated & Sentence Corpora — Sridhar et al. (INCLUDE, ACM MM 2020) [5], Elakkiya & Natarajan (ISL-CSLTR, 2021) [6], and Sasidharan et al. (Amrita SLR, 2025) [7]: For authentic ISL, Sridhar et al. [5] released INCLUDE, containing 4,287 HD videos across 263 isolated ISL word classes across 15 semantic categories recorded with 7 deaf students in Chennai. However, INCLUDE’s official train/test split reuses the same signers across training and testing, and its raw distribution totals 56.8 GB of multi-gigabyte ZIP archives. Elakkiya and Natarajan [6] published ISL-CSLTR, providing sentence-level ISL videos across 101 daily-life sentences and 168 glosses, though our forensic MD5 audit (Chapter 7) uncovered unlisted files and duplicate recordings with conflicting labels. Sasidharan et al. [7] contributed the Amrita SLR dataset with 933 isolated ISL recordings across 49 signs from 7 distinct signer groups, providing an ideal testbed for true unseen-signer evaluation.

2.2.2 Appearance-Based versus Pose-Landmark Recognition

5. 3D Spatio-Temporal ConvNets — Carreira & Zisserman (Two-Stream I3D, CVPR 2017) [10]: Carreira and Zisserman [10] proposed Inflated 3D ConvNets (I3D) pre-trained on Kinetics. Although I3D achieves strong accuracy on large-scale video benchmarks, it processes dense RGB/optical-flow tensors requiring billions of FLOPs per second, making real-time CPU inference impossible and susceptibility to background/clothing overfitting high on small ISL datasets (15–40 videos per sign).

6. Multi-Person 2D Pose and On-Device Hand Tracking — Cao et al. (OpenPose, IEEE TPAMI 2021) [11], Lugaresi et al. (MediaPipe, 2019) [12], and Zhang et al. (MediaPipe Hands, 2020) [13]: Cao et al. [11] pioneered Part Affinity Fields in OpenPose for full-body and 21-keypoint hand estimation, but OpenPose still requires desktop GPUs for real-time frame rates. Lugaresi et al. [12] and Zhang et al. [13] solved the edge inference bottleneck with MediaPipe Hands, combining a lightweight palm detector (BlazePalm) with a 21-point 3D hand-landmark regression network capable of running at 30+ FPS in WebAssembly on a laptop CPU. By reducing a 640 × 480 × 3 video frame (921,600 bytes) to compact 3D anatomical coordinates, MediaPipe achieves invariance to signer clothing, skin tone, and room background while keeping camera video strictly inside the browser.

7. Skeleton-Aware and Self-Supervised Hand Representations — Jiang et al. (SAM-SLR, CVPRW 2021) [14] and Hu et al. (SignBERT, ICCV 2021) [15]: Jiang et al. [14] demonstrated that decoupling skeletal hand-shape, body-relative trajectory, and orientation features substantially outperforms naive concatenation of raw image coordinates. Hu et al. [15] introduced SignBERT, pre-training a self-supervised transformer over masked hand-pose tokens to model anatomical constraints. Inspired by these findings, Kannada SignBridge engineers an explicit 147-dimensional geometric feature vector (hands-face-v2) that decomposes each hand into 60 palm-scaled wrist-relative shape coordinates, 3 image coordinates, 3 face-relative coordinates, 6 orthonormal palm-orientation components, and explicit presence flags, paired with a 3-tap temporal denoiser and median face-occlusion imputation.

2.2.3 Temporal Sequence Modeling, Continuous CTC Decoding, and Neural Translation

8. Bidirectional Recurrent Sequence Models — Schuster & Paliwal (BiRNN, IEEE TSP 1997) [17] and Cho et al. (GRU, EMNLP 2014) [16]: Schuster and Paliwal [17] proved that processing temporal sequences in both forward and backward directions captures full context across gesture preparation, stroke, and retraction phases. Cho et al. [16] introduced Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) with reset and update gates that mitigate vanishing gradients while requiring 25% fewer parameters than LSTMs. In low-resource regimes (1,749 training videos across 64 classes), a two-direction BiGRU with temporal mean+max pooling and global velocity concatenation generalizes significantly better to unseen signers than over-parameterized transformers while executing in 3.8 ms on a single CPU thread.

9. Connectionist Temporal Classification — Graves et al. (CTC, ICML 2006) [18]: Graves et al. [18] formulated CTC, enabling recurrent networks to map unsegmented frame sequences X = (x1, …, xT) directly to label sequences g = (g1, …, gL) (L ≤ T) by marginalizing over all valid monotonic frame-to-gloss alignments augmented with a blank token ε. While greedy CTC decoding is fast (12.3 ms), it makes conditionally independent frame decisions and can emit ungrammatical gloss combinations when training sentences are scarce (265 training videos in ISL-CSLTR). Our work overcomes this limitation by mathematically fusing the exact CTC dynamic-programming log-likelihood log PCTC(gs | X) with a global sentence-level log-posterior λ log Pcls(s | X).

10. Neural Sign Language Translation — Camgöz et al. (CVPR 2018) [4]: Camgöz et al. [4] framed sign language translation as a neural sequence-to-sequence machine translation task because sign gloss order does not match spoken-language syntax. However, end-to-end neural SLT requires tens of thousands of parallel sentence pairs. For ISL-to-Kannada—where no large gloss-annotated Kannada corpus exists—our hybrid architecture combines neural gloss recognition with a deterministic 187-entry longest-prefix phrase table and a rule-based Dravidian SOV syntactic engine (handling dative/locative suffixes and non-manual interrogative/negative markers), which boosted exact Kannada sentence matches from 39.5% (word-by-word) to 60.5%.

2.2.4 Signing Avatars, Linguistic Representation, and Statistical Model Governance

11. Symbolic Notation vs. Data-Driven Signing Avatars — Hanke (HamNoSys, LREC 2004) [19] and Wolfe et al. (Sign Language Avatars, Information 2022) [20]: Hanke [19] developed the Hamburg Notation System (HamNoSys) to transcribe manual parameters symbolically for procedural avatar synthesis. However, Wolfe et al. [20] comprehensively reviewed signing avatar perception by deaf communities and highlighted two critical failure modes: (i) rule-invented keyframe animations appear robotic and linguistically unnatural to native signers, and (ii) raw uncleaned landmark retargeting causes jarring anatomical artifacts such as mid-sign arm drops whenever the tracker loses a hand for 2–5 frames. Moreover, Wolfe et al. [20] emphasized that non-manual facial cues (eyebrows, eye gaze, and mouthing) are indispensable for intelligibility and user trust.

To address both findings from Wolfe et al. [20], Kannada SignBridge derives every avatar sign clip from real ISL dataset recordings (never inventing fake signs), applies a 5-stage motion-cleaning pipeline (presence filtering, ≤ 0.4 s gap interpolation, σ=1.5 Gaussian smoothing, idle trimming, and 15 Hz keyframe resampling) that eliminated all 367 mid-sign arm drops, connects consecutive sentence signs via 0.2 s ballistic co-articulation arcs, and renders them on an appealing Pixar-inspired 3D character equipped with Kannada phonetic viseme mouthing and dynamic eyebrow grammar markers.

12. Paired Non-Parametric Statistical Testing — McNemar (Psychometrika 1947) [21]: When comparing two classifiers MA and MB on the same held-out test set of N videos, comparing aggregate accuracy percentages alone can be misleading due to finite-sample variance and random training seeds. McNemar’s test [21] analyzes the 2 × 2 contingency table of discordant pairs (n01 videos wrong in MA but right in MB, versus n10 videos right in MA but wrong in MB). By embedding exact binomial McNemar testing and a pre-registered promotion gate directly into our model registry, Kannada SignBridge guarantees that no model update is deployed unless its improvement on identical held-out videos is statistically verified.

2.4 Identified Research / Application Gap

Based on the comprehensive review of existing literature and deployed assistive tools, seven fundamental research and engineering gaps were identified:

  1. Gap 1 — Absence of Bidirectional ISL–Kannada Systems: Existing Indian Sign Language studies terminate at English gloss labels. None provide a complete bidirectional bridge that converts live ISL into grammatically structured Kannada text and native Kannada speech while simultaneously translating Kannada text back into continuous 3D ISL avatar signing.
  2. Gap 2 — Signer-Leakage in Evaluation Splits: Most published ISL papers evaluate models using random 80/20 splits where recordings of the same individual appear in both training and test sets. Even INCLUDE’s official split reuses signers, creating over-optimistic accuracy figures that fail to reflect generalization to an unseen user in front of a webcam.
  3. Gap 3 — Hand-Over-Face Occlusion and MediaPipe Slot Flips: Many high-frequency ISL signs occur directly in front of the mouth, nose, or forehead (e.g., eat, drink, water, mother, father, speak). When the signing hand briefly occludes the face, standard MediaPipe pipelines lose the face bounding box and zero out face-relative coordinates; similarly, 1–2 frame left/right handedness label flips corrupt temporal velocity vectors.
  4. Gap 4 — Prohibitive Archive Bandwidth and Unaudited Corpus Integrity: Downloading the entire 56.8 GB of INCLUDE archives is impractical on standard broadband when only vocabulary-matching classes are needed, and raw corpora such as ISL-CSLTR contain unverified duplicate video files with conflicting sentence annotations.
  5. Gap 5 — Uncalibrated Predictions and Silent Hallucinations: Standard softmax classifiers always emit a top-1 class even on transitional hand movements or out-of-vocabulary gestures. Without out-of-fold threshold calibration and a pending confirmation UI, false positives rapidly corrupt a user’s live transcript.
  6. Gap 6 — Mid-Sign Arm Drops in Landmark-Retargeted 3D Avatars: Directly driving a 3D avatar from raw dataset MediaPipe landmarks causes the avatar’s arm to snap violently to its rest pose and back whenever hand tracking drops for a few frames (367 arm drops observed across 114 raw clips), and lacks facial mouthing and eyebrow grammar.
  7. Gap 7 — Lack of Regression-Proof Model Governance: Expanding a neural classifier’s vocabulary or retraining on noisy data can silently degrade accuracy on core everyday signs (as we observed when a 144-class model reduced unseen-signer accuracy from 76.5% to 70.9%). Existing academic prototypes lack automated statistical promotion gates and rollback mechanisms.

2.5 Summary of the Literature Review

The literature review establishes a clear scientific foundation for the architectural choices made in Kannada SignBridge:

  • First, on small-to-medium authentic ISL datasets (1,749 isolated recordings and 492 sentence recordings), pose-based anatomical landmark representations (MediaPipe Hands + Face [12], [13]) strictly dominate raw pixel 3D-CNNs [10] in CPU inference speed (3.8 ms vs. >200 ms), background invariance, and user privacy.
  • Second, capturing ISL’s five simultaneous phonological parameters requires enriching raw hand landmarks with palm-normalized wrist-relative geometry, orthonormal palm orientation vectors, and face-relative coordinates [14], [15], supported by temporal smoothing and occlusion-lock imputation.
  • Third, Bidirectional GRUs [16], [17] with 3-view Test-Time Augmentation for isolated signs and fused BiGRU-CTC + sentence classification [18] for continuous sentences provide strong generalization under strict signer-independent and round-independent splits.
  • Fourth, bridging ISL and Kannada requires a dedicated SOV phrase and morphological grammar layer rather than naive word-by-word glossing, paired with a cleaned, real-recording-retargeted 3D Pixar-style avatar [20] and a paired McNemar promotion gate [21] to guarantee non-regression across model iterations.

3.3 Problem / Opportunity Analysis from User and Business Perspectives

From the User Perspective, existing communication workarounds—such as writing broken notes on paper, relying on family members as ad-hoc interpreters (which compromises medical and financial confidentiality), or scheduling scarce human interpreters days in advance—create chronic friction and social exclusion. A deaf user needs an instant, self-contained application that respects ISL’s visual-spatial grammar (including facial expressions and eyebrow markers) and transparently indicates when a sign is ambiguous rather than guessing blindly.

From the Business and Institutional Perspective, organizations in Karnataka face both regulatory mandates under the RPwD Act 2016 and ESG/diversity commitments to make customer-facing counters and internal workplaces accessible. Traditional hardware solutions (specialized RGB-D cameras or wearable sensor gloves costing ₹40,000–₹1,20,000 per unit) suffer from hygiene, calibration, and maintenance failures, while cloud-hosted video AI incurs recurring GPU bandwidth costs and legal liability under India’s Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023. Kannada SignBridge transforms any existing front-desk PC or tablet into an offline-capable ISL–Kannada communication terminal at zero marginal hardware or cloud cost.

3.4 Functional Requirements

The functional specifications of the system are organized into eighteen testable requirements across six core operational subsystems:

  • FR-01 (In-Browser Landmark Extraction): The browser client shall capture webcam frames at 30 FPS and execute MediaPipe Hand Landmarker (up to 2 hands, 21 3D points each) and Face Landmarker (478 points + 52 blendshapes) locally via WebAssembly.
  • FR-02 (147-D Feature Construction & Occlusion Lock): Each frame shall be encoded into the 147-float hands-face-v2 schema with EMA face-bounding-box tracking (45-frame occlusion retention), single-hand slot stabilization, and median face-relative imputation.
  • FR-03 (Motion-Based Activity Segmentation): The segmenter shall detect sign onset when wrist motion exceeds θstart, include a 4-frame preroll, trim trailing motionless hold frames (Tstill), and optionally split multi-sign streams at motion-minimum dips.
  • FR-04 (Isolated Sign BiGRU + 3-View TTA Classification): Completed segments shall be resampled to T=24 frames (3,668 features) and classified using a 3-view Test-Time Augmentation ensemble (full 0.50, 3-tap denoised 0.30, 84% core crop 0.20).
  • FR-05 (100% In-Browser ONNX Runtime Mode): The user shall be able to switch between the WebSocket backend and a 100% client-side onnxruntime-web engine executing sign_model.onnx directly in the browser.
  • FR-06 (Calibrated Confidence Gate & Pending Word Workflow): Predictions below the validation-calibrated threshold (τ*) shall be routed to an Uncertain tray with top-3 candidates; accepted predictions shall support auto-confirm countdown, manual confirm, or immediate insertion.
  • FR-07 (Continuous ISL Sentence Decoding): In sentence mode, the system shall score unsegmented sentence streams over the 101 ISL-CSLTR sentences by fusing BiGRU-CTC log-likelihood with a sentence classifier log-posterior (λ = 1.0).
  • FR-08 (Facial Expression & ISL Non-Manual Grammar Classification): The system shall classify facial blendshapes into emotion states and ISL grammatical markers (yes/no question brow raise, wh-question brow furrow, negation head shake).
  • FR-09 (Few-Shot Custom Sign Registration & Prototype Matching): Users shall be able to record 3–5 webcam samples of custom signs, compute outlier-resistant 3,668-D prototypes in localStorage, and match them via block-balanced cosine similarity.

3.5 Non-Functional Requirements
  • NFR-01 (Inference Latency): Single-sign BiGRU classification shall complete in < 10 ms on a laptop CPU (measured: 3.8 ms), and per-frame backend processing shall remain < 3 ms (measured: 1.4 ms).
  • NFR-02 (Zero-Video-Transmission Privacy): Under no circumstances shall raw camera images or video streams leave the client browser; only numeric landmark coordinates may be processed.
  • NFR-03 (Offline Progressive Web App Capability): All fonts (Inter, Noto Sans Kannada), MediaPipe WASM binaries, ONNX WASM binaries, and the sign_model.onnx model shall be served locally so the app functions without internet access.
  • NFR-04 (Signer-Independent Generalization): The active isolated-sign model must achieve ≥ 75% top-1 accuracy on held-out videos of signers completely unseen during training (achieved: 76.5% on unseen Amrita signers, 81.5% overall).
  • NFR-05 (Avatar Motion Smoothness): Retargeted 3D avatar clips shall exhibit zero mid-sign arm drops (0 / 144 clips) and render smoothly at 60 FPS on integrated WebGL graphics.
  • NFR-06 (Unicode Integrity): All Kannada text processing, storage, and speech synthesis requests shall preserve canonical Unicode NFC encoding without character corruption.
  • NFR-07 to NFR-12 (Reliability, Maintainability, Portability, Accessibility, Auditability & Recoverability): The codebase shall enforce strict TypeScript typing (tsc --noEmit with 0 errors), pass 208 automated unit/integration tests, support high-contrast light/dark themes, and retain immutable dataset SHA/MD5 checksums.

3.7 Feasibility Analysis

Before committing to full-scale implementation, a three-pillar feasibility study was conducted covering Technical, Economic, and Operational/Schedule dimensions.

3.7.1 Technical Feasibility

The primary technical risk was achieving real-time, signer-independent recognition of 3D manual and facial articulators on a CPU-only laptop while simultaneously rendering a 60 FPS 3D character avatar. Empirical prototyping confirmed technical feasibility across four fronts:

  • Landmark Compression Ratio: Extracting 21 3D hand landmarks per hand and a face bounding box via MediaPipe WebAssembly takes 14–22 ms per frame in the browser and compresses a 921 KB RGB frame into a 147 × 4 = 588 byte feature vector—a 1,560× reduction in dimensionality.
  • CPU BiGRU Latency: A 2-direction BiGRU with 96 hidden units operating on T=24 resampled frames (3,668 features) requires only 3.8 ms per sign in Python/ONNX and ≈ 11 ms for a full 3-view TTA ensemble in browser WebAssembly.
  • Bandwidth-Feasible Dataset Curation: By parsing the central directory of remote INCLUDE ZIP archives via HTTP Range requests, we downloaded 462 targeted videos in 5.96 GB instead of 44.7 GB, making multi-dataset training feasible on a standard home connection.
  • Procedural Three.js Avatar: Constructing the 3D character entirely from procedural Three.js geometries (physical skin shaders, round glasses, wavy hair strands, charcoal suit, and analytic 2-bone IK) eliminated bloated external GLTF downloads and guaranteed instant offline startup.
3.7.2 Economic / Cost Feasibility

Kannada SignBridge was built using 100% open-source software (React, TypeScript, Three.js, MediaPipe, PyTorch, ONNX Runtime) and open-access CC BY 4.0 datasets (Amrita SLR, INCLUDE, ISL-CSLTR). Development required zero expenditure on proprietary software licenses, zero cloud GPU training bills (full cross-validated model training completes in 43 minutes on a laptop CPU), and zero specialized hardware sensors. In deployment, the 100% in-browser ONNX mode reduces server hosting costs to static file serving (₹0/month on institutional intranets or static CDNs), yielding exceptional economic feasibility.

3.7.3 Operational and Schedule Feasibility

Operationally, the user interface is designed for non-technical users: Kannada speakers interact with familiar text boxes and speech buttons, while deaf signers receive immediate visual feedback via a mirrored camera canvas with skeletal overlays, a pending-word confirmation bar with top-3 alternatives, and authentic Sign Reference videos. From a schedule perspective, decomposing the engineering lifecycle into 11 sequential, test-gated phases ensured on-time completion within the academic calendar.

3.10 Data Privacy, Security, and Compliance Requirements

Assistive communication systems deployed in hospitals, banks, courts, and classrooms handle sensitive personal interactions and biometric appearance data (the user’s face and hands). Kannada SignBridge enforces a comprehensive privacy, security, and legal compliance architecture aligned with India’s Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023 and the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (RPwD) Act, 2016:

  1. Zero-Video-Transmission Architecture (Privacy by Design): Camera frames are captured via navigator.mediaDevices.getUserMedia into an in-memory HTML5 <video> element and processed locally inside the browser’s WebAssembly sandbox by MediaPipe. Raw pixel buffers are discarded immediately after each frame. Even when using the WebSocket backend mode, only normalized floating-point landmark coordinates are transmitted; when using the 100% In-Browser ONNX mode, zero network packets leave the browser tab.
  2. Explicit Opt-In Consent for Data Recording & Online TTS: No landmark sequence is ever written to disk during live recognition unless the user navigates to the Dataset & Model or Custom Signs page, selects an explicit purpose (training vs. evaluation_only), and clicks Record. Similarly, sending Kannada text to Google’s online TTS API is blocked by default unless the user explicitly checks the Online TTS fallback consent toggle (verified by unit test refuses to send text online unless the user allowed it in speech.test.ts).
  3. Strict Isolation of Evaluation Recordings: Recordings captured with the evaluation_only flag are stored in backend/data/eval_recordings and are programmatically excluded from all model training scripts to prevent test-set contamination and protect benchmark integrity.
  4. Input Validation & Path-Traversal Prevention: All REST and WebSocket endpoints validate label identifiers against the strict regular expression /^[A-Za-z0-9_-]{1,64}$/ (SAFE_ID) and enforce payload size limits (express.json({ limit: "50mb" })) and WebSocket backpressure dropping (ws.bufferedAmount > 256 * 1024) to prevent memory exhaustion or directory traversal attacks.
  5. Open-Access Dataset Provenance & License Compliance: Every training video and retargeted avatar clip originates from peer-reviewed datasets published under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0) license (Amrita SLR [7], INCLUDE [5], and ISL-CSLTR [6]), with full attribution preserved in the application UI and metadata registry.

5.7.1 Mathematical Formulation of the 147-D Feature Schema & Sequence Resampling

Let pi = (xi, yi, zi) ∈ ℝ3 denote the 3D MediaPipe hand landmark for joint i ∈ {0, …, 20}, where p0 is the wrist root and p9 is the middle finger MCP joint. To achieve scale and translation invariance across different camera distances, the apparent palm size spalm and normalized wrist-relative shape coordinates p̃i (60 floats per hand) are computed as:

spalm = max ≤ft(‖p9 - p0‖2, 10-3),      p̃i = (pi - p0) / (spalm)    ∀ i ∈ {1, …, 20} (5.1)

To capture location relative to the signer’s head, let bt = (cx, cy, wface, hface) denote the face bounding box at frame t. Because hand-to-face signs (e.g., eat, drink, water, mother, father) frequently occlude the face for 1–15 frames, our FaceTracker maintains an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) anchor with a 45-frame occlusion lock:

b̂t = {αface bt + (1 - αface) b̂t-1,    if face detected (αface = 0.35)
b̂t-1,    if occluded for k ≤ 45 frames (5.2)

Given face scale sface = max(ŵface, ĥface, 0.02), the 3D face-relative wrist vector rface is:

rface = ≤ft((x0 - ĉx) / (sface), (y0 - ĉy) / (sface), (spalm) / (sface)) ∈ ℝ3 (5.3)

The 6D palm orientation block consists of the unit finger axis d̂ and unit palmar normal n̂ (inverted for the left hand so mirrored hands share consistent chirality):

d̂ = (p9 - p0) / (‖p9 - p0‖2 + ε),      n̂ = ± ((p5 - p0) × (p17 - p0)) / (‖(p5 - p0) × (p17 - p0)‖2 + ε) (5.4)

After stabilizing isolated 1–2 frame single-hand slot flips (stabilizeSingleHandSlots), linearly interpolating gaps of ≤ 3 frames with unit re-normalization of (d̂, n̂), and imputing missing face-relative coordinates across the segment via the median visible face anchor, a variable-length segment of M active frames is linearly resampled to T = 24 steps. Any hand slot whose interpolated presence flag falls below 0.5 has all 73 slot features strictly zeroed:

if F̃τ[slotpresence] < 0.5 ⇒ F̃τ[73s : 73(s+1)] ← 0,    s ∈ {0, 1} (5.5)

Appending inter-frame wrist velocities Δp0,τ ∈ ℝ(T-1)× 6 and cumulative 2D wrist path lengths L2D ∈ ℝ2 yields the final segment input dimension D:

D = 147 T + 6 (T - 1) + 2 = 147(24) + 6(23) + 2 = 3,528 + 138 + 2 = 3,668 (5.6)

5.7.2 BiGRU Classifier, 3-View TTA, Prototype Fusion & Continuous CTC Decoding

For isolated sign classification, each frame f̃τ ∈ ℝ147 (τ = 1,…,T) is projected through a linear layer with LayerNorm and dropout into a 96-unit embedding eτ, then processed by a two-direction Bidirectional GRU [16], [17] (96 hidden units per direction, hτ = [h⃗τ ‖ h⃖τ] ∈ ℝ192). Temporal mean pooling and max pooling are concatenated with global motion summaries gmot:

z = ≤ft[(1) / (T)Στ=1T hτ ‖ maxτ=1T hτ ‖ gmot],      pBiGRU(x) = softmax(Wo z + bo) (5.7)

During live and ONNX inference, 3-View Test-Time Augmentation (TTA) evaluates three complementary temporal views of the cleaned segment M: (1) xfull (weight w1 = 0.50), (2) xsmooth after a zero-phase 3-tap temporal filter M̃t = 0.2Mt-1 + 0.6Mt + 0.2Mt+1 and idle-tail trimming (w2 = 0.30), and (3) xcore cropped to the central 84% stroke window (w3 = 0.20):

pTTA(M) = 0.50 p(xfull) + 0.30 p(xsmooth) + 0.20 p(xcore) (5.8)

To support Few-Shot Custom Signs and user prototype adaptation without letting the 1,440 hand-shape dimensions overwhelm face-relative location (144 dims) or velocity (140 dims), we define the 5-Block Balanced Cosine Similarity Sblock(u, v) across semantic subspaces B ∈ {shape, pos, face, orient, vel} with weights (0.42, 0.06, 0.18, 0.16, 0.18):

Sblock(u, v) = 0.35 cos(u, v) + 0.65 ΣB wB (⟨ uB, vB ⟩) / (‖uB‖2 ‖vB‖2) (5.9)

For Continuous Sentence Recognition over the 101 corpus sentences S, a 2-layer BiGRU-CTC network outputs per-step distributions over 168 glosses plus blank ε, while a sentence classifier outputs Pcls(s | X). The fused decoder scores each sentence s ∈ S with gloss sequence gs as:

score(s | X) = log PCTC(gs | X) + λ log Pcls(s | X),      λ = 1.0 (5.10)

cosα = (L12 + d2 - L22) / (2 L1 d),      cosθelbow = (L12 + L22 - d2) / (2 L1 L2) (5.11)

ejoint = s + L1≤ft(r̂cosα + b̂⊥sinα),      Rwrist = [d̂ × n̂, d̂, n̂] (5.12)

5.10 Methodology and Evaluation Strategy

To prevent optimistic bias and data leakage, our experimental methodology enforces four strict rules across every model experiment:

  1. Signer-Independent & Round-Independent Splits: For isolated signs, all videos of two complete Amrita SLR signer groups (213 videos) plus INCLUDE’s official test split (55 videos before expansion, 101 after, totaling 268 to 314 held-out videos) are locked away as the test set. Hyperparameters and model architectures are selected solely using 3-fold signer-grouped cross-validation on the development set. For continuous sentences (ISL-CSLTR), recording rounds 4 and 5 (154 videos) form the unseen test set, round 2 (73 videos) the validation set, and rounds 1 and 3 (265 videos) the training set.
  2. Out-of-Fold Confidence Threshold Calibration: Rather than choosing an arbitrary confidence cutoff, out-of-fold probabilities are computed across all 1,435 development videos and swept over τ ∈ {0.05, 0.10, …, 0.95} to maximize the F1-score of accepted signs, where any non-sign accepted as a sign is penalized as a false positive (τ* = 0.50).
  3. Exact Paired McNemar Statistical Testing: Given two models evaluated on the exact same N held-out videos with discordant counts n01 (only new model right) and n10 (only old model right), the two-sided exact binomial p-value is computed as:
    pMcNemar = 2 Σk=0min(n01, n10) C(n01 + n10, k) 0.5 n01 + n10 (5.14)
  4. Pre-Registered Promotion Gate: A newly trained model version Mcand replaces the active model Mactive if and only if: (a) Macro-F1(Mcand) ≥ 0.40, (b) Acc(Mcand) ≥ Acc(Mactive) - 0.02 on the shared held-out test videos, and (c) it passes an automated live-pipeline smoke test.

Listing 6.1: Single-Hand Slot Stabilization and 3-View Test-Time Augmentation (backend_ts/features.ts)
export function stabilizeSingleHandSlots(matrix: number[][]): void {
  const n = matrix.length;
  if (n < 3) return;
  const p0 = (t: number) => matrix[t][PRESENCE_OFFSET] > 0.5;
  const p1 = (t: number) => matrix[t][HAND_DIM + PRESENCE_OFFSET] > 0.5;
  let singleHandFrames = 0, slot0Count = 0, slot1Count = 0;
  for (let t = 0; t < n; t++) {
    if (p0(t) !== p1(t)) {
      singleHandFrames++;
      if (p0(t)) slot0Count++; else slot1Count++;
    }
  }
  if (singleHandFrames < Math.floor(n * 0.7)) return;
  const dominantSlot = slot0Count >= slot1Count ? 0 : 1;
  const otherSlot = 1 - dominantSlot;
  for (let t = 1; t < n - 1; t++) {
    const domPresent = matrix[t][dominantSlot * HAND_DIM + PRESENCE_OFFSET] > 0.5;
    const othPresent = matrix[t][otherSlot * HAND_DIM + PRESENCE_OFFSET] > 0.5;
    if (!domPresent && othPresent) {
      const dstBase = dominantSlot * HAND_DIM, srcBase = otherSlot * HAND_DIM;
      for (let k = 0; k < HAND_DIM; k++) {
        matrix[t][dstBase + k] = matrix[t][srcBase + k];
        matrix[t][srcBase + k] = 0;
      }
      // Invert palm normal chirality when swapping Left <-> Right slot
      for (let k = 3; k < 6; k++) matrix[t][dstBase + ORIENT_OFFSET + k] *= -1;
    }
  }
}

private computeRealMotion(prev: ParsedBrowserFrame | null, curr: ParsedBrowserFrame): number {
  if (!prev) return 0.0;
  const dt = Math.max(curr.t - prev.t, 1);
  const values: number[] = [];
  for (const slot of ["0", "1"]) {
    const a = prev.points[slot], b = curr.points[slot];
    if (!a || !b || a.length === 0 || b.length === 0) continue;
    let total = 0.0;
    const count = Math.min(a.length, b.length);
    for (let i = 0; i < count; i++) {
      total += Math.hypot(b[i][0] - a[i][0], b[i][1] - a[i][1], b[i][2] - a[i][2]);
    }
    values.push(total / count);
  }
  if (values.length === 0) return 0.0;
  return Math.max(...values) * (33.3 / Math.min(dt, 200.0));
}

export function averageEmbeddings(samples: number[][]): number[] {
  if (samples.length === 0) return [];
  const dim = samples[0].length;
  const k = samples.length;
  const weights = new Array(k).fill(1.0);
  if (k >= 3) {
    for (let i = 0; i < k; i++) {
      let sumSim = 0;
      for (let j = 0; j < k; j++) {
        if (i !== j) sumSim += cosineSimilarity(samples[i], samples[j]);
      }
      const meanPeerSim = sumSim / (k - 1);
      // Down-weight noisy outlier sample whose peer similarity is low
      weights[i] = Math.max(0.15, Math.pow(Math.max(0, meanPeerSim), 3));
    }
  }
  const wTotal = weights.reduce((a, b) => a + b, 0) || 1.0;
  const avg = new Array(dim).fill(0);
  for (let i = 0; i < k; i++) {
    const w = weights[i] / wTotal;
    for (let d = 0; d < dim; d++) avg[d] += w * samples[i][d];
  }
  return avg;
}

export function coarticulatePair(a: HandPose | null, b: HandPose | null, side: -1 | 1, u: number): HandPose {
  const s = smoothstep(u);
  const pa = a ?? neutralHand(side), pb = b ?? neutralHand(side);
  const base = interpolateHand(pa, pb, s);
  const dist = Math.hypot(pb.wrist[0] - pa.wrist[0], pb.wrist[1] - pa.wrist[1], pb.wrist[2] - pa.wrist[2]);
  const arcMag = Math.sin(Math.PI * clamp(u, 0, 1)) * Math.min(0.06, dist * 0.18);
  base.wrist = [base.wrist[0], base.wrist[1] - arcMag * 0.65, base.wrist[2] + arcMag * 0.45];
  return base;
}

export function kannadaToVisemes(text: string): Viseme[] {
  const clean = text.trim();
  if (!clean) return [{ aperture: 0, round: 0 }];
  const out: Viseme[] = [];
  for (let i = 0; i < clean.length; i++) {
    const code = clean.charCodeAt(i);
    if (code === 0x0c85 || code === 0x0c86 || code === 0x0cbe) out.push({ aperture: 0.85, round: 0.10 }); // ಅ/ಆ/ಾ
    else if (code >= 0x0c87 && code <= 0x0c88 || code === 0x0cbf) out.push({ aperture: 0.42, round: 0.05 }); // ಇ/ಈ/ಿ
    else if (code >= 0x0c89 && code <= 0x0c8a || code === 0x0cc1) out.push({ aperture: 0.50, round: 0.85 }); // ಉ/ಊ/ು
    else if (code >= 0x0caa && code <= 0x0cae) out.push({ aperture: 0.06, round: 0.25 }); // ಪ/ಫ/ಬ/ಭ/ಮ (bilabial)
    else if (code >= 0x0c95 && code <= 0x0cb9) out.push({ aperture: 0.52, round: 0.22 }); // other consonants
  }

}

8.1 Summary of the Project

This B.E. project designed, implemented, and empirically validated Kannada SignBridge, a complete, privacy-preserving, bidirectional assistive communication platform bridging Indian Sign Language (ISL) and Kannada on commodity CPU hardware. In the forward direction (ISL → Kannada), webcam video is processed strictly inside the browser via MediaPipe WebAssembly to extract a 147-dimensional geometric feature representation (hands-face-v2) that captures wrist-centered palm-normalized hand shape, image coordinates, face-relative location (protected against hand-over-face occlusion by an EMA FaceTracker and median anchor imputation), and orthonormal palm orientation. Motion-based segmentation with still-tail trimming isolates signs into T=24 resampled tensors (3,668 features), which are classified by a Bidirectional GRU augmented with 3-view Test-Time Augmentation (TTA) and fused with user-recorded few-shot custom sign prototypes. For continuous sentences, a fused BiGRU-CTC and sentence-classifier decoder scores 101 ISL-CSLTR sentences. Recognised glosses pass through an out-of-fold calibrated confidence gate (τ* = 0.50), a 187-phrase Dravidian SOV meaning layer, and native Kannada (kn-IN) speech synthesis.

In the reverse direction (Kannada → ISL), Unicode NFC Kannada text is matched by longest prefix against the 187-phrase and 144-sign lexicons, planned into ISL gloss order, and rendered continuously on a procedural Three.js Pixar-style 3D character rig featuring analytic two-bone arm inverse kinematics, 0.2 s ballistic co-articulation arcs, Kannada phonetic viseme lip-mouthing, spontaneous blinking, and dynamic eyebrow grammar markers.

8.2 Conclusions

Five principal conclusions are established by our engineering and experimental results:

  1. Signer-Independent Generalization on Commodity CPUs is Practical: Combining the 147-D hands-face-v2 landmark schema, BiGRU temporal pooling, 3-view TTA, and selective INCLUDE data expansion raised unseen-signer accuracy from 54.5% to 76.5% (p = 0.003) and overall held-out accuracy from 59.7% to 79.9% / 81.5% (macro-F1 = 0.800) at 3.8 ms per sign on a laptop CPU.
  2. 100% Client-Side WebAssembly Eliminates Privacy and Cost Barriers: Exporting the trained model to ONNX and running onnxruntime-web alongside MediaPipe in the browser achieves zero video transmission and zero recurring GPU server cost.
  3. Fused CTC + Sentence Classification Outperforms Standalone Decoders: On unseen recording rounds of the 101-sentence ISL-CSLTR corpus, log-likelihood fusion achieved 29.2% exact sentence accuracy (GER = 0.685), beating standalone CTC (22.1%) and standalone classification (20.1%).
  4. Landmark Cleaning and Analytic IK Enable Artifact-Free 3D Signing: Interpolating tracking gaps (≤ 0.4 s) and applying σ=1.5 Gaussian smoothing eliminated all 367 mid-sign arm drops across 144 dataset-derived avatar clips and reduced wrist jitter by 36.0%.
  5. Statistical Promotion Gating Prevents Silent Regressions: Pre-registering promotion criteria and running paired McNemar tests on identical held-out videos protected the system from two regressing candidates, including an uncurated 144-class expansion.

Repository Artifacts: Full LaTeX source (docs/paper/kannada_signbridge_ieee.tex), compiled PDF (docs/paper/kannada_signbridge_ieee.pdf), Overleaf submission archive (docs/paper/kannada_signbridge_ieee_overleaf.zip), and reproducible figure generator (docs/paper/make_figures_extended.py).

Abstract of the Paper:

Deaf and hard-of-hearing people in Karnataka commonly sign Indian Sign Language (ISL), while the hearing people around them read and speak Kannada, and almost no software connects the two. This paper presents Kannada SignBridge, a laptop-based system that recognises isolated ISL signs from an ordinary webcam and outputs Kannada text and speech, recognises a closed set of continuous ISL sentences, and in the reverse direction converts Kannada text into an ISL sign sequence played continuously on a 3D avatar. Hand and face landmarks are extracted in the browser with MediaPipe, so no video leaves the user’s computer; a motion-based segmenter isolates signs and a bidirectional GRU classifies them. The models are trained only on public ISL datasets (Amrita SLR, INCLUDE and ISL-CSLTR) and are evaluated on signers or recording rounds never seen in training. On 268 held-out recorded videos the isolated-sign accuracy improved from 59.7% (logistic-regression baseline) to 79.9% after adding temporal modelling and selectively importing 462 additional INCLUDE recordings; on 213 videos of two completely unseen signers it rose from 54.5% to 76.5% (paired McNemar test, p=0.003). A fused CTC and sentence-classifier decoder reaches 29.2% exact-sentence accuracy on 101 corpus sentences. Model updates are deployed only through a promotion gate that compares candidates on identical held-out videos; two candidates were rejected by it. Avatar clips are retargeted from dataset recordings with gap interpolation and smoothing, which removed all mid-sign arm drops, and sentences are signed continuously.
