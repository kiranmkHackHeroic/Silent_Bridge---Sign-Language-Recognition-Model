\noindent\textbf{\textit{Abstract:}} Deaf and hard-of-hearing citizens in Karnataka communicate primarily in Indian Sign Language (ISL), whereas clinics, banks, and schools operate in Kannada, with virtually no bidirectional software connecting the two on commodity hardware. We present \textit{Silent Bridge}, a laptop-based, privacy-preserving system that recognizes isolated ISL signs and closed-set continuous sentences from a standard webcam into Kannada text and speech, supports browser-local few-shot custom sign enrollment, and translates Kannada text into continuous ISL signing on a 3D avatar. Hand and face landmarks (147-D schema \texttt{hands-face-v2}) are extracted in-browser via MediaPipe so raw video never leaves the client. Trained solely on public ISL corpora (Amrita SLR, INCLUDE, ISL-CSLTR) and evaluated strictly on unseen signers or rounds, BiGRU modeling with selective HTTP-range INCLUDE expansion raised 64-sign accuracy on 268 identical held-out videos from 59.7\% to 79.9\% (81.5\% on 314 test videos; 76.5\% on 213 unseen Amrita signers; McNemar $p = 0.003$). Scaling vocabulary $2.25\times$ to 144 signs (3,182 videos) initially failed our non-regression promotion gate with a single seed (unseen Amrita 70.9\%), but a 3-seed BiGRU ensemble resolved the variance, reaching 87.2\% overall (457/524 videos, macro-F1 0.850), 84.7\% on the shared 314-video set, and 81.7\% on unseen Amrita signers. Fused BiGRU-CTC + sentence-classifier decoding achieves 29.2\% exact accuracy across 101 unseen-round sentences (GER 0.685). On the reverse path, trajectory cleaning and analytic two-bone inverse kinematics eliminated all 367 mid-sign arm drops across 144 dataset-derived clips while reducing mean and 90th-percentile wrist jitter by 36.0\% and 43.8\%. We present a five-axis roadmap for scaling beyond 144 signs and a pre-registered $N = 8$--$12$ Deaf ISL user study protocol, reporting zero fabricated human scores.

\noindent\textbf{\textit{Keywords:}} Indian sign language, Kannada translation, MediaPipe landmarks, bidirectional GRU, signing avatar, assistive technology

\section{Introduction}
Sign languages are independent natural languages with spatial grammar and non-manual markers (NMMs), not manual encodings of spoken languages \cite{li2020wlasl,joze2019msasl}. Although Indian Sign Language (ISL) serves a large deaf community across India, automatic sign language recognition (SLR) has focused primarily on American and German sign languages \cite{li2020wlasl,joze2019msasl,koller2015phoenix,camgoz2018slt}, leaving regional pairs such as ISL--Kannada unsupported. Because certified ISL interpreters are scarce in Karnataka, everyday clinical, banking, municipal, and classroom interactions remain inaccessible.

\textit{Silent Bridge} enforces five engineering constraints: authentic publicly licensed ISL data only, strict browser-side video processing so only landmark vectors cross the network, CPU-only laptop execution, signer- or round-independent evaluation with negative results reported, and automated non-regression promotion governance. Our main contributions are: (i) a privacy-preserving bidirectional ISL--Kannada pipeline running locally via ONNX WebAssembly or FastAPI; (ii) selective HTTP-range INCLUDE ingestion (up to 86.7\% bandwidth reduction) and a promoted 144-sign 3-seed BiGRU ensemble achieving 87.2\% overall and 81.7\% unseen-signer accuracy; (iii) fused BiGRU-CTC + classifier sentence decoding with seed-variance diagnostics; and (iv) a cleaned 3D signing avatar eliminating 100\% of mid-sign arm drops ($367 \rightarrow 0$), paired with a five-axis vocabulary scaling roadmap and a pre-registered Deaf ISL user study protocol.

\section{Literature Review}
Large isolated-sign benchmarks such as WLASL \cite{li2020wlasl} and MS-ASL \cite{joze2019msasl} and continuous translation corpora such as RWTH-PHOENIX-Weather \cite{koller2015phoenix,camgoz2018slt} target Western sign languages. For ISL, INCLUDE \cite{sridhar2020include} provides 4,287 videos across 263 isolated words recorded in Chennai, ISL-CSLTR \cite{elakkiya2021islcsltr} provides 101 gloss-annotated continuous sentences across five recording rounds, and Amrita SLR \cite{amrita2025slr} provides multi-signer isolated recordings from Kerala. Recent benchmarks such as ISLTranslate \cite{joshi2023isltranslate} and iSign \cite{joshi2024isign} align continuous ISL videos with English text without frame-level gloss annotations.

End-to-end 3D convolutional video networks \cite{carreira2017i3d} demand GPUs and large datasets. Pose-based models using OpenPose \cite{cao2021openpose} or MediaPipe \cite{lugaresi2019mediapipe} compress video by over $10^3\times$ and generalize more reliably across lighting and background shifts, while compact Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Units (BiGRUs) \cite{cho2014gru} trained with Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) \cite{graves2006ctc} execute in real time on laptop CPUs. On the synthesis side, symbolic-notation avatars frequently receive low Deaf-community acceptance due to robotic motion and absent NMMs \cite{wolfe2022avatars}. Unlike prior ISL studies that rely on random signer-leaking splits and English-only output, \textit{Silent Bridge} enforces unseen-signer evaluation, bidirectional Kannada support, and strict non-regression governance.

\section{Datasets and Methodology}
\subsection{Validated Corpora, Selective Ingestion, and MD5 Integrity Audit}
All training and evaluation data come exclusively from authentic public ISL corpora under CC BY 4.0 (Table~\ref{tab:datasets}), with ground-truth labels taken strictly from official dataset metadata rather than filenames. Our 64-sign baseline uses 1,749 videos (49 Amrita, 42 INCLUDE, 27 shared; 17--45 videos per sign), and our promoted 144-sign model expands to 3,182 videos (2,658 development, 524 held-out test; 49 Amrita, 122 INCLUDE). Seven disjoint Amrita signer groups are reconstructed from naming conventions; two unseen signers (213 videos) plus INCLUDE's official test split form the held-out test set (268 initial, 314 shared core, 524 full). Model selection uses 3-fold signer-grouped cross-validation on development data only, and paired comparisons use the exact two-sided McNemar test.

Because INCLUDE spans 56.8 GB of ZIP archives, we inspect remote ZIP central directories via HTTP Range requests to fetch only target sign classes and verify CRC-32 checksums, reducing transfer volume by 86.7\% in Phase 1 (5.96 vs. 44.7 GB for 462 videos) and 61.8\% in Phase 2 (18.59 vs. 48.7 GB for 1,430 videos) with zero corruptions. For ISL-CSLTR, we split strictly by recording round (rounds 1 and 3 train: 265 videos; round 2 validation: 73; unseen rounds 4--5 test: 154). A cryptographic MD5 audit of all 687 files verified 492/492 folder-name labels, cataloged 195 unlisted videos, and quarantined 9 duplicate files across 5 duplicate groups (4 with conflicting labels).

\subsection{Bidirectional Pipeline and 147-D Geometric Landmarks}
As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:arch}, the React 18 / TypeScript client executes MediaPipe Hand and Face Landmarkers (v0.10.21, matching offline dataset extraction) directly inside the browser and sends only 147-D landmark vectors to a FastAPI backend---or runs an exported 3.1 MB BiGRU locally via \texttt{onnxruntime-web} WASM SIMD. Recognized glosses pass through a 187-entry ISL-to-Kannada SOV meaning layer with \texttt{kn-IN} speech synthesis. In reverse, Unicode NFC Kannada text undergoes agglutinative suffix stripping, longest-phrase matching, SOV-to-ISL reordering, and 60 fps Three.js avatar playback; unsupported words are explicitly disclosed and never guessed.

Each frame $\mathbf{f}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{147}$ (\texttt{hands-face-v2}) encodes both hands ($2 \times 73$) plus face visibility $v_{\text{face}}$. Per hand, 20 non-wrist joints $\mathbf{p}_i$ are centered on wrist $\mathbf{p}_0$ and scaled by palm size $s_{\text{palm}} = \max(\|\mathbf{p}_9 - \mathbf{p}_0\|_2, 10^{-3})$ (60 dims), concatenated with image wrist coordinates (3), face-relative wrist vector $\mathbf{r}_{\text{face}}$ scaled by face width and height (3, with an upper-center high-resolution face-crop fallback), unit finger-axis and palm-normal vectors $(\hat{\mathbf{d}}, \hat{\mathbf{n}})$ (6), and hand presence (1):
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_i = \frac{\mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{p}_0}{s_{\text{palm}}},\quad
\mathbf{r}_{\text{face}} = \left(\frac{p_{0,x}-c_x}{w_{\text{face}}},\,\frac{p_{0,y}-c_y}{h_{\text{face}}},\,p_{0,z}-c_z\right),\quad
D = 147\,T + 6\,(T - 1) + 2 = 3{,}668 \;(T=24).
\end{equation}

\subsection{BiGRU Classification, Seed Ensembling, and Threshold Calibration}
We benchmark multinomial logistic regression, a 2-layer BiGRU \cite{cho2014gru} (96-D LayerNorm-ReLU projection, 96 hidden units per direction, mean/max temporal pooling concatenated with motion statistics $\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^{398}$), a LogReg+BiGRU blend, and a 3-seed BiGRU ensemble (\texttt{gru\_seeds}). All BiGRUs train with AdamW, class-weighted cross-entropy, early stopping, and $5\times$ landmark augmentation (temporal crops up to 20\%, Gaussian coordinate jitter, and left--right mirroring). At inference, multi-view Test-Time Augmentation (TTA) averages probabilities over raw, smoothed, and core-cropped views. Out-of-fold (OOF) development predictions calibrate the confidence threshold $\tau^*$ maximizing accepted F1 (Figure~\ref{fig:cal}: $\tau^* = 0.50$ for 64 signs and $\tau^* = 0.55$ for 144 signs); sub-threshold predictions are surfaced as ``uncertain'' alongside their top-3 alternatives.

\subsection{Live Segmentation, Continuous Sentence Fusion, and Few-Shot Enrollment}
During live webcam use, a sign segment begins when inter-frame wrist speed $m_t$ exceeds $\theta_{\text{start}}$ and ends upon a stillness pause, 200 ms of hand absence, or a 4 s ceiling. Trimming trailing still frames ($m_k < \theta_{\text{still}}$) prior to classification increased live replay accuracy on 268 test videos from 136 to 184 correct signs. For continuous sentences (101 corpus sentences, 168 glosses), a 2-layer BiGRU (128 units/direction, $2\times$ temporal stride) emits CTC gloss posteriors \cite{graves2006ctc} fused with a 32-step logistic sentence classifier ($\lambda = 1.0$ tuned on round-2 validation):
\begin{equation}
\text{score}(s) = \log P_{\text{CTC}}(g_s \mid \mathbf{X}) + \lambda \log P_{\text{cls}}(s \mid \mathbf{X}),\qquad
S_{\text{block}}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}) = \sum_{b=1}^{5} w_b \frac{\langle \mathbf{u}^{(b)}, \mathbf{v}^{(b)} \rangle}{\|\mathbf{u}^{(b)}\|_2 \|\mathbf{v}^{(b)}\|_2 + 10^{-8}}.
\end{equation}
Sentences with softmax posterior below 0.50 are flagged uncertain with top-3 candidates. For user-defined regional signs (3--5 webcam clips without retraining), $S_{\text{block}}$ computes block-balanced cosine similarity across $B = 5$ semantic subspaces so 120-D handshape cannot drown out 6-D face-relative cues.

\subsection{3D Avatar Trajectory Cleaning, Analytic IK, and Model Governance}
Each avatar clip is retargeted directly from an authentic ISL recording: wrist positions are anchored to the face, depth is estimated from palm scale, and arm joint angles are solved analytically via two-bone Inverse Kinematics ($L_1 = 0.29\text{ m}$, $L_2 = 0.27\text{ m}$):
\begin{equation}
\cos\theta_{\text{elbow}} = \text{clip}\!\left(\frac{L_1^2 + L_2^2 - d^2}{2\,L_1\,L_2},\; -1,\; 1\right),\qquad h(u) = 10u^3 - 15u^4 + 6u^5.
\end{equation}
Our trajectory cleaner discards tracks with under 15\% visibility, linearly interpolates dropout gaps up to $0.40\text{ s}$, Gaussian-smooths ($\sigma = 1.5$) wrist paths, orientations, and finger curls, and trims motionless margins to $0.12\text{ s}$ before resampling at $\le 15\text{ fps}$. Consecutive sentence signs blend across $0.20\text{ s}$ via minimum-jerk quintic spline $h(u)$ with an outward clearance arc. Under our model governance gate, candidates are promoted only if held-out macro-F1 $\ge 0.40$, shared-test accuracy does not regress by more than 0.02, and reload/live smoke tests pass.

\section{Experimental Results}
\subsection{Isolated Signs and Scaling from 64 to 144 Classes}
Table~\ref{tab:isolated} and Figure~\ref{fig:results} summarize held-out isolated-sign accuracy. On 268 identical test videos, BiGRU modeling with face-crop extraction raised accuracy from 59.7\% (LogReg) to 72.4\%, and Phase-1 INCLUDE expansion lifted it to 79.9\% (unseen Amrita: $54.5\% \rightarrow 76.5\%$; exact paired McNemar $p = 0.003$, 34 improved vs. 13 degraded). On the 314-video core test set, the 64-sign BiGRU achieved 81.5\% (macro-F1 0.800) and lowered live replay SER from 0.485 to 0.274 (54 substitutions, 8 deletions, 24 insertions). Residual errors concentrate in phonologically close pairs sharing facial anchors (\textit{EAT/NOSE}, \textit{TEETH/NOSE}, \textit{GOOD EVENING/GOOD MORNING}), and across 50 concatenated 5-sign streams, stillness segmentation achieved 0.474 SER with pauses versus 0.857 without lowering hands.

Expanding to 144 signs (3,182 videos) with a single-seed BiGRU scored 85.2\% on 210 new INCLUDE videos, but degraded shared 314-video accuracy to 76.1\% and unseen Amrita to 70.9\%; our promotion gate rejected it. Training a 3-seed BiGRU ensemble (\texttt{gru\_seeds}, \texttt{v20261103-195651}) eliminated this variance and passed promotion: shared 314-video accuracy rose from 81.5\% to \textbf{84.7\%} (macro-F1 $0.800 \rightarrow 0.846$), unseen Amrita rose from 76.5\% to \textbf{81.7\%} (+5.2 pts), INCLUDE (311 videos) reached \textbf{91.0\%}, and full 524-video Top-1 reached \textbf{87.2\%} (457/524, macro-F1 \textbf{0.850}).

\subsection{Perturbation Robustness and Browser--Python Parity}
Applying landmark perturbations to the 268 held-out videos (Figure~\ref{fig:robust}, LogReg 66.8\% clean) shows that horizontal mirroring, 20--40\% hand dropout, 0.67--1.5$\times$ speed shifts, scale, and jitter alter accuracy by $\le 1.5$ points, whereas hiding the face drops accuracy to 47.8\% ($-19.0$ pts). Browser MediaPipe WASM versus offline Python extraction across 8 videos yielded 0.002--0.007 median landmark distance, 91.8--100\% hand-presence agreement, and 7/8 identical top-1 predictions.

\subsection{Continuous Sentence Decoding and Seed Variance}
Table~\ref{tab:sentences} evaluates continuous decoders across 154 unseen-round ISL-CSLTR test videos (101 sentences, chance $\approx 1\%$). Fused BiGRU-CTC + sentence-classifier decoding achieves \textbf{29.2\%} exact accuracy and the lowest Gloss Error Rate (\textbf{0.685}; 37.6\% round 4, 18.8\% round 5). A candidate adding 187 unlisted videos dropped to 22.1\% and was rejected (19 vs. 8 paired wins; McNemar $p = 0.052$). Multi-seed runs revealed 9.1-pt seed variance (20.1--29.2\%), with the candidate lower on every seed (mean $-5.8$ pts) due to premature early stopping (27--51 vs. 81--87 epochs); moving unlisted videos into training only recovered to 25.3\%.

\subsection{Meaning Layer, Objective Avatar Audit, and Laptop CPU Latency}
Across 124 held-out streams covering 31 multi-sign phrases (SER 0.301; 60.5\% exact sign sequences), our ISL-to-Kannada SOV meaning layer raised exact Kannada reference match from 39.5\% (word-by-word glossing, chrF 0.605) to \textbf{60.5\%} (chrF \textbf{0.669}). On the reverse path, all 144 dataset-derived avatar clips (146 audited variants; 161/162 lexicon entries covered) were rebuilt with trajectory cleaning. Table~\ref{tab:avatar} shows mid-sign arm drops fell from 367 (114 clips) to \textbf{0}, while mean, median, and 90th-percentile wrist jitter fell by \textbf{36.0\%}, \textbf{26.3\%}, and \textbf{43.8\%}. Table~\ref{tab:latency} confirms CPU-only real-time operation (3.8--15.3 ms/sign; 37 ms sentence decode).

\section{Scalability and Deaf Community Evaluation}
\subsection{Vocabulary Scalability Roadmap (144 to 500--2,000+ Signs)}
Our $64 \rightarrow 144$ sign expansion proved that scaling ISL recognition is constrained less by CPU compute than by signer-diversity imbalance and single-model variance, which 3-seed ensembling overcame (+5.2 pts on unseen Amrita). Scaling further to 500--2,000+ signs follows five architectural axes: (1) \textit{metric-learning prototype retrieval} using an ArcFace/contrastive backbone ($d = 192$) and HNSW cosine index for $O(1)$ regional enrollment; (2) \textit{phonological sub-unit factorization} (handshape, facial anchor, orientation, movement heads) plus procedural fingerspelling for proper nouns; (3) \textit{signer-balanced ingestion} via HTTP-range acquisition across all 263 INCLUDE classes, iSign \cite{joshi2024isign}, and ISLTranslate \cite{joshi2023isltranslate} gated by Karnataka signers; (4) \textit{sub-millisecond edge scaling} where expanding the final $O(C\cdot d)$ head from $C = 144 \rightarrow 1{,}000$ adds only $\approx 0.16\text{M}$ MACs ($<0.4\text{ ms}$ on WASM SIMD); and (5) \textit{open-vocabulary CTC beam search with a Kannada/ISL-gloss LM} paired with automated avatar cleaning.

\subsection{Pre-Registered Deaf ISL User Study Protocol}
Objective kinematic audits (Table~\ref{tab:avatar}) verify smooth trajectories but cannot substitute for human perceptual evaluation by fluent Deaf signers \cite{wolfe2022avatars}. Accordingly, the repository tags every generated clip as \texttt{"unverified"}, and we report \textbf{zero fabricated human scores}. Following institutional ethics approval, we pre-register a $N = 8$--$12$ Deaf ISL user study in Bengaluru (20--25 min per session with an accredited ISL interpreter) structured around three tasks: \textit{Task 1 (Blind Isolated Understandability)} presents 18 randomized avatar clips without text to measure open-ended identification (\%), 4-AFC accuracy (\%), 5-point visual clarity, and structured error tags (handshape, orientation, location, missing NMM, speed); \textit{Task 2 (Sentence Comprehension)} evaluates 5 Kannada-to-ISL avatar sentences (3--5 signs) on gist accuracy (\%) and 1--5 naturalness; and \textit{Task 3 (Bidirectional Usefulness \& Usability)} conducts a live public-counter dialogue scored via 5-point usefulness ratings, the 10-item System Usability Scale (SUS, 0--100), and interpreter-mediated qualitative feedback.

\section{Conclusion}
\textit{Silent Bridge} delivers a privacy-preserving, CPU-real-time bidirectional ISL--Kannada translation system evaluated strictly on unseen signers under automated non-regression governance. Scaling from 64 to 144 signs via a 3-seed BiGRU ensemble reached 87.2\% overall accuracy (81.7\% on unseen Amrita signers), fused BiGRU-CTC decoding reached 29.2\% across 101 unseen-round sentences, and trajectory cleaning eliminated all 367 avatar arm drops while reducing 90th-percentile jitter by 43.8\%. Future work will execute the pre-registered Deaf ISL user study, collect Karnataka signer corpora for metric-learning vocabulary expansion, and add facial blendshape NMMs. The authors thank Dr. Beemsha Arya (Principal, BMSCE), Dr. R Ashok Kumar (HoD, CSBS), and the creators of Amrita SLR, INCLUDE, and ISL-CSLTR.
